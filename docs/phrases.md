# Everyday phrases

| Norwegian | English | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hvordan har du det? | How are you? | General check-in |
| Går det bra med deg? | Are you OK? | Natural, often concerned |
| Er alt bra med deg? | Is everything OK with you? | Checking on someone |
| Har du det bra? | Are you doing well? | Well-being |
| Jeg hører på deg. | I'm listening to you. | Attentive listening |
| Jeg savner deg. | I miss you. | Personal |
| Jeg prøver å lære norsk. | I'm trying to learn Norwegian. | Learning |
| Jeg kommer til å savne deg. | I'm going to miss you. | Expected future |
| Jeg kommer alltid til å savne deg. | I will always miss you. | Future + `alltid` |
| Jeg kommer til å savne deg for alltid. | I'll miss you forever. | `for alltid` |
| Ute på tur, aldri sur! | Out on a hike, never grumpy! | Rhyming Norwegian saying about enjoying the outdoors; elliptical expression |
| Å ta seg vann over hodet | To get in over one's head / bite off more than one can chew | Idiom; `Jeg har tatt meg vann over hodet` = I've gotten in over my head |
| Det er plankekjøring! | It's a piece of cake! | Informal expression for something very easy |
| Det koster å være kar. | It costs to be a big shot / Keeping up appearances isn't cheap. | Saying about the expense of living stylishly or maintaining status; often humorous |
| Man skal yte for å nyte. | You have to put in effort to enjoy the rewards. | Proverb; `yte` = contribute / make an effort, `nyte` = enjoy |

| Hvorfor sitte inne når alt håp er ute? | Why stay inside when all hope is lost? | Dark wordplay: `ute` means “outside,” while `alt håp er ute` means “all hope is lost”; infinitive `sitte` is natural here |

**Går det bra med deg?** is often more concerned than the general greeting
**Hvordan har du det?** Context and tone matter.

**Jeg vil alltid savne deg** is also grammatical, but **vil** can express
willingness or intention as well as a future prediction. See [verbs](verbs.md).
