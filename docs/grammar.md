# Grammar

## Subjects are normally required

Norwegian normally expresses the subject of a finite clause, as English does.

| Norwegian | English | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jeg snakker norsk. | I speak Norwegian. | Explicit subject |
| Det regner. | It is raining. | Dummy subject `det` |
| Kom hit! | Come here! | Imperatives omit the subject |
| Savner deg! | Miss you! | Informal message fragment |

Do **not** generalize informal fragments into a rule that subjects can routinely be dropped.

## V2: the finite verb is second in main declarative clauses

- **Jeg snakker norsk i dag.** — I speak Norwegian today.
- **I dag snakker jeg norsk.** — Today I speak Norwegian.
- **I dag jeg snakker norsk** is not normal main-clause word order.

Yes/no questions commonly put the finite verb first: **Snakker du norsk?**

## Nouns and articles

| Gender | Indefinite | Definite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Masculine | en bil | bilen |
| Feminine | ei bok | boka |
| Neuter | et hus | huset |

Bokmål also permits common-gender forms such as **en bok / boken**.

## Adjectives

Attributive adjectives normally precede the noun:

- **en stor bil** — a big car
- **et stort hus** — a big house
- **store biler** — big cars

Predicative adjectives follow linking verbs: **Bilen er stor.** — The car is big.
